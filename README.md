# Seeds

A small, source-first exploration of Black demographics in the United States.

Serve the directory over HTTP so the Three.js module can load:

```sh
python3 -m http.server 4173
```

Then open `http://localhost:4173`. Each card leads with a sourced quote and archival portrait, followed by a sourced demographic fact. Refreshes advance through a daily shuffled sequence; after all ten cards, it reshuffles and continues without a daily cap. The facts are curated snapshots, not live Census API results. Select a labeled branch, a 3D marker, or use the arrow controls to browse manually.

## Sources and definitions

The fact set links to Census, CDC/NCHS, BJS/FBI, NCES/NAEP, peer-reviewed research, and BlackDemographics sources. Quotes link to their cited speeches or texts; portraits link to Wikimedia Commons file pages with credit and license notes. Source notes keep the population, denominator, and year visible. The family branch distinguishes household arrangements in the 2019 CPS from self-reported fatherhood prevalence in the 2015–2019 NSFG; neither alone establishes what share of births are concentrated among a share of unique fathers.

The chart section uses bars for group comparisons and labels each measure's population, denominator, and year. It does not infer multiple-partner fertility from household arrangements, treat homicide victims as offenders, or present SNAP participant composition as a race-specific participation rate. Paternity Court episodes are not used to calculate race-specific paternity outcome rates because no verified public dataset was located that pairs episode test outcomes with participants' self-identified race; inferring race from names or appearance and generalizing from TV-selected cases would be unreliable. The FY 2023 USDA SNAP race/ethnicity figures show each category's share of all participants; race/ethnicity was voluntary and unknown or missing for 17 percent, and the figures are national rather than area-specific. The FBI offender-victim cross-tab is limited to 2019 single-victim/single-offender incidents and does not identify law-enforcement status. A separate 2019 chart compares 2,574 FBI Black-victim/Black-offender cases with 294 Black people in Mapping Police Violence's database; the FBI count excludes other incident types, and the sources have different coverage, so these are counts rather than complete, comparable population rates. When session storage is available, the Wall rotates through 20 sourced entries in a shuffled no-repeat cycle, distinguishing guilty pleas, convictions, civil findings, and reported financial controversies. Entries cover public-fund cases and crimes against identified Black victims, each limited to the named person or organization; they do not generalize conduct to a racial group. The Tyrone Brooks entry links to DOJ records and reporting on his guilty plea and sentence. The BLM Global Network Foundation entry summarizes AP reporting about an organization-owned property; it is not a court finding or proof that founders personally misused donations. No portrait is shown unless a reusable image license is verified. Marriage and divorce charts show current marital-status prevalence in the 2024 ACS, not annual event rates. The multiple-partner fertility estimates are from an overall young-women cohort and are not Black-specific; the Gallup identity chart uses approximate thresholds reported for racial and ethnic groups, not exact estimates or measures of attraction or behavior. Religion charts use Pew's 2023–24 Religious Landscape Study, distinguishing affiliation from self-reported monthly participation; death-rate comparisons use NCHS's age-adjusted 2024 rates corrected for race and Hispanic-origin misclassification.

The earnings comparison uses ACS 2024 Table B20017 medians for people age 16 and older who worked full-time, year-round, in 2024 inflation-adjusted dollars; it is not household income or hourly pay, and estimates are subject to sampling error. The industry chart uses ACS Table B24030 to show selected industries as a share of employed men or women age 16 and older; it is an overall U.S. comparison, not race-specific. The 2024 birth chart uses NCHS's general fertility rates: births per 1,000 women ages 15–44. Its non-Hispanic single-race categories and Hispanic-any-race category follow the source report's classifications.

Economic charts add ACS 2024 educational attainment and median household income, plus Federal Reserve 2022 family net worth. Household income, earnings, and net worth have different universes and should not be read as interchangeable measures of buying power. Debt, business survival, and hiring/DEI cards identify what the cited public data can and cannot establish; the site does not infer trip or cosmetic-procedure spending from anecdotes, business longevity from business counts, or hiring advantage from workforce totals. The wrongful-conviction chart limits the cited sevenfold estimate to wrongful murder convictions and does not generalize it to all crimes or specifically to men. The voting-history card separates access to the ballot from party preference and notes the historical shifts that make a single party-alignment series misleading.

The justice offense chart uses U.S. Sentencing Commission FY 2025 figures for the race composition of people sentenced in selected federal offense categories. These are not conviction rates or crime rates; the denominators are sentenced federal cases, not all arrests, prosecutions, or people of each race. The adult-literacy chart uses NCES PIAAC 2023 proficiency levels, not a binary literacy rate or an IQ measure, and notes the survey's race-category and response-rate limits. No race-based IQ ranking is presented; PIAAC measures literacy, numeracy, and adaptive problem solving, not IQ.
