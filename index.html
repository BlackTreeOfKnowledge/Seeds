ASSESSMENT · IQ DATA LIMIT

IQ and literacy are not interchangeable measures

This project does not present an IQ ranking by race as a measure of group or innate ability.

For directly assessed adult skills Use literacy, numeracy, and problem-solving measures with their methods and limits attached

PIAAC measures literacy, numeracy, and adaptive problem solving; it is not an IQ test. Race-based IQ averages can be misleading when presented without test design, sampling, language, education, and social context. The literacy chart above reports an actual assessed skill rather than substituting IQ for it.

Source · NCES, what PIAAC measures Methods · NCES 2023 PIAAC results and limitations

ECONOMY · PERSONAL DEBT

Debt types are measurable; lifestyle spending is not a proxy

Mortgages, vehicle loans, education loans, and credit-card balances are tracked in household finance surveys. A representative race-specific chart of trips or cosmetic procedures is not available from those measures.

Cars, homes, education, travel, and elective procedures are different financial questions Do not infer spending or debt from stereotypes or anecdotes

The SCF measures family assets and liabilities, including debt categories, but does not estimate spending on named activities such as group trips or BBL surgery. A debt comparison needs a defined balance, borrower/household denominator, and survey year.

Source and methods · Federal Reserve Survey of Consumer Finances

ECONOMY · BLACK-OWNED BUSINESSES

Ownership, jobs, and survival need separate measures

Census business surveys report employer businesses and paid employees by owner demographics, but a survival rate needs a defined firm cohort followed over time.

Black-owned firms and Black women-owned firms Do not treat business counts as job counts or survival rates

A useful comparison should distinguish employer firms from nonemployer businesses, count paid employees rather than owners, and identify the cohort and years used for survival. The available tables are not a single race-and-sex-specific longitudinal series, so no longevity percentage is inferred here.

Source · Census Annual Business Survey tables Census · Nonemployer Statistics

ECONOMY · EMPLOYMENT & DEI

Employment rates do not tell us who gets hired more easily

Employment, unemployment, labor-force participation, hiring rates, and pay gaps are different measures.

Black men and women compared with other groups Hiring advantage cannot be inferred from workforce totals alone

The earnings and industry charts above do not measure applicant-to-hire outcomes or isolate the effects of DEI programs. A fair hiring comparison needs comparable jobs, applicant pools, qualifications, geography, and time period; pay should be compared separately from hiring.

Source · Bureau of Labor Statistics, employment status by race, sex, and age

SAFETY · WRONGFUL MURDER CONVICTIONS

A “seven times” finding—with a narrower scope

A 2017 study estimated the relative likelihood of wrongful murder conviction for Black people compared with White people.

White people1×
Black people7×

This is the study’s estimated relative risk for wrongful murder convictions, based on exoneration data—not a rate for all crimes, all convictions, or Black men specifically. Exonerations are documented cases and do not capture every wrongful conviction; the index is not a percentage.

Source · Gross et al., “Race and wrongful convictions in the United States,” PNAS (2017) Data context · National Registry of Exonerations

VOTING · PARTY HISTORY

Black political alignment is not one unbroken party line

The history runs from exclusion from the ballot, through Reconstruction and Jim Crow, to federal voting-rights protections and later party realignment.

Slavery and disenfranchisement → Reconstruction → Jim Crow → Voting Rights Act → modern coalitions A single “Democratic Party since slavery” chart would erase major changes

Enslaved people were denied the vote, and later legal rights were repeatedly suppressed. Party labels and coalitions changed over time; a responsible history separates access to the ballot from party preference and uses election-specific evidence.

Source · National Archives, 15th Amendment Source · National Archives, Voting Rights Act of 1965

DOCUMENTED CONVICTION · BALTIMORE

Catherine Pugh

Pugh pleaded guilty to federal fraud and tax offenses and was sentenced to 36 months in prison. Prosecutors said the Healthy Holly book scheme included about $800,000 in payments from the University of Maryland Medical System.

This case involved a book-sale fraud and Baltimore institutions; it does not establish that Black residents as a whole were the sole or direct victims.

U.S. DOJ · Pugh sentencing and case details

Entries cover public-fund misconduct and crimes with identified victims; each concerns only the named person or organization. Financial reporting is not a court finding, and no entry represents a racial group.
